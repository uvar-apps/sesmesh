Dear Editors,

I submit the manuscript ``\sesmesh: watertight, deterministic meshes of the
exact solvent-excluded surface, from peptides to virus capsids'' for
consideration as a research article in the Journal of Molecular Graphics and
Modelling.

The solvent-excluded surface (SES) is the standard model of molecular shape:
it is used to visualize macromolecules, to measure their area, volume and
internal cavities, and as the dielectric boundary in continuum
electrostatics, which needs a closed, manifold triangulation. Programs that
triangulate it trade accuracy, robustness and scale:
analytic methods are exact but fragile in singular configurations, and grid
methods are robust but only approximate the surface. The manuscript presents
a method, and its open-source implementation, that keeps the robustness of a
grid without giving up the exact surface:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item the exact SES distance function is sampled only in a sparse narrow band
  of a uniform grid, selected by provably conservative bounds;
\item Marching Cubes with a uniform face-ambiguity rule yields closed,
  consistently oriented, edge-manifold meshes, and every vertex is refined
  onto the exact surface with its analytic normal, patch type and nearest
  atom;
\item internal cavities are classified exactly by signed volume, and the
  output is byte-identical for any number of threads.
\end{itemize}

The validation covers \val{corpus-n} structures (\val{corpus-atoms-min} to
\val{corpus-atoms-max} atoms), whose mesh area and volume differ from their
$h \to 0$ extrapolation by \val{area-mean-0.25}\,\% and
\val{vol-mean-0.25}\,\% on average at $h = 0.25$\Ang{}, with $O(h^2)$
convergence; \val{sweepk-ok} of \val{sweepk-n} randomly drawn Protein Data
Bank entries; a fuzzing campaign of the input readers; and a comparison with
six programs (MSMS, NanoShaper, ESES, ChimeraX, EDTSurf and sesa), using the
same atoms and radii wherever a program accepts them. A 2.44-million-atom
virus capsid is meshed at 1\Ang{} in \val{large-3J3Q-seconds}\,s on a
10-core laptop and in \val{x86-3J3Q-1}\,s on an AMD Zen~5 16-core system. The discussion draws conclusions on what exact vertex
placement changes in the error budget of a grid method, and on the limits
that remain.

The work falls within the journal's scope of the study of molecular shape
and properties, and it addresses the journal's emphasis on reproducibility:
\sesmesh{} is free software (MIT or Apache-2.0), and version 1.1.2, the
version the manuscript describes, is archived on Zenodo together with the
validation data the manuscript uses
(\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24238837}{doi:10.5281/zenodo.24238837}).
The manuscript sources and the script that turns these data into its tables
and plots are in the public repository.

The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under
