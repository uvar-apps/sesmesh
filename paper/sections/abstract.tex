\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The solvent-excluded surface (SES), or Connolly surface, is a widely used
model of a macromolecule's boundary with its solvent. Programs that
triangulate it trade accuracy, robustness and scale: analytic methods are
exact but fragile in singular configurations, while grid methods are robust
but accurate only to the voxel size. We present \sesmesh{}, a Rust library
and command-line tool that meshes the \emph{exact} SES on a uniform grid. It
samples the SES distance function of Quan and Stamm (2016) only in a sparse
narrow band selected by provably conservative bounds. Marching Cubes with a
uniform face-ambiguity rule yields closed, consistently oriented,
edge-manifold meshes; every vertex is refined onto the exact surface, with
its analytic normal, patch type and nearest atom. Internal cavities are
classified exactly by signed volume. On \val{corpus-n} structures
(\val{corpus-atoms-min} to \val{corpus-atoms-max} atoms) the mesh area and
volume differ from their $h \to 0$ extrapolation by \val{area-mean-0.25}\,\%
and \val{vol-mean-0.25}\,\% on average at $h = 0.25$\Ang, with $O(h^2)$
convergence; \val{sweepk-ok} of \val{sweepk-n} random Protein Data Bank
entries produced valid meshes. NanoShaper and ESES, which also place
vertices on the exact surface, agree with \sesmesh{} to
\val{cmp-nanoshaper-darea-max-0.25}\,\% in area, while MSMS, ChimeraX and
EDTSurf show failures, non-manifold or open meshes, or errors of several per
cent. The output is byte-identical for any thread count. A
2.44-million-atom virus capsid is meshed at 1\Ang{} in
\val{large-3J3Q-seconds}\,s on a 10-core laptop and \val{x86-3J3Q-1}\,s on
an AMD Zen~5 16-core system. \sesmesh{} is free software.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} solvent excluded surface; molecular surface;
Marching Cubes; mesh generation; deterministic parallelism; Rust.
